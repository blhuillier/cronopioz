<div class="text"><p>Presented as recovered artifacts from a near-future Mars outpost, The Martian Gardener Project constructs an archive of traces—images, objects, and documents offered as evidence of a botanical life once sustained beyond Earth. Mars is framed not as a frontier for expansion, but as an experimental stage that resists mastery: a site to reconsider the fragility of our tether to the living world, and how accidental vitality can still take hold.</p><p>Drawing on astrobiology and planetary research, the project weaves fact and fiction to imagine a rhythm of cultivation that transcends mere survival. Glass-held remnants, speculative botanical plates, trial records, and bio-inspired sensing mechanisms map intimate, sustained attempts and adjustments to nurture connection and make room for unforeseen variations.</p><p>Here, cultivation appears not as a technical feat, but as a ritual of continuity. Care is practiced across distance, keeping Earth’s life within reach. The project stays with the fissures where worn records and new growth coexist, tracing the seams where our relation to the world is tested and reshaped.</p></div>
<div class="text"><p class="exhibited">Exhibited in <em>Verdant Echoes: Martian Gardener Project</em> (solo), CICA Museum, Gimpo, 2025.</p></div>
<hr>
<div class="text work-head"><h2>Suspended Bloom: Post-Mortem Meditations</h2><p class="work-meta">2025–2026 · Distressed glass, botanical remnants, red clay, paprika powder, adhesive, epoxy resin, spray paint, monofilament, stainless steel pipe</p><p>Glass-encased remnants of iterative botanical experiments, suspended forms shaped by cycles of trial, decay, and renewal. Each holds the echo of growth rehearsed and relinquished.</p><p>Low brown noise runs through the room, almost breath-like, suggesting the thin hum of a Martian atmosphere. Projected footage drifts across the glass surfaces like a shifting archive of dust, light, and disrupted currents. Together they create a field of unstable perception, where remnants waver between preservation and dissolution, and memories hover in spectral stillness.</p></div>

<div class="text work-head"><h2>Martian Morphologies: Field Notes from Sector R (‘Red Garden’)</h2><p class="work-meta">2025 · 270 × 120 cm · Archival pigment print (iterative hand–digital layering)</p><p>Fragmentary records of synthetic and self-adapting flora, shaped by forces beyond control. From a speculative cultivation zone on Mars, where design drifts into unexpected forms, and life roots itself on its own terms.</p><p>Each species begins as a story. The works adopt the visual grammar of scientific botanical plates, then interrupt it. Hand-drawn sketches and handwritten fragments are digitized, distorted, and worked back into the surface, producing a specimen that never fully stabilizes. Each plate tracks a form under pressure, recording what shifts and takes hold. The series asks how harsh constraints reshape care, and how life persists by rewriting the systems meant to contain it.</p></div>

<div class="text"><h3>Notes on Process</h3><p>An image builds slowly through layering, omissions, and afterimages that accumulate each time the work moves across media. Sketches that grew from stories are photographed and scanned, then deconstructed, reconfigured, and distorted in cycles of printing and physical reworking, until traces of translation solidify into form.</p></div>

<div class="text work-head"><h2>Tumblemesh</h2><p class="work-meta">2025 · 50 × 50 × 50 cm · Botanical remnants, PLA filament, mineral pigment · Collaboration with Dan Mikesell</p><p>Tumblemesh drifts with the Martian wind, unspooling a weave of organic and synthetic strands. It sheds micro-nodes along its path that register light, temperature, and chemistry. Its tumbling body leaves a diffuse, reactive web in its wake, attuned to the shifting terrain.</p></div>
